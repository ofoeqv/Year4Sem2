# Question 16 - Normalised Frequency and Samples per Cycle

## Category
Theory

## Topic
Sampling and normalised frequency

## Question
What is the normalised frequency in samples per cycle of a 100 Hz sine wave sampled at a sample frequency of 1 kHz?

## Options
A. 0.1

B. 2π

C. 10

D. 0.2π

## Correct Answer
C. 10

## Explanation
The number of samples per cycle is:

\[
\frac{f_s}{f}
\]

Here:

\[
f_s = 1000\text{ Hz}, \quad f = 100\text{ Hz}
\]

So:

\[
\frac{1000}{100} = 10
\]

Therefore, there are 10 samples per cycle.

## Important note
This question uses the wording samples per cycle. Under that wording, the answer is 10.

If the question instead asked for normalised frequency in cycles per sample, the answer would be:

\[
\frac{f}{f_s} = \frac{100}{1000} = 0.1
\]

If it asked for normalised angular frequency in radians per sample, the answer would be:

\[
\Omega = 2\pi \frac{f}{f_s} = 0.2\pi
\]

## Must-know concept
Samples per cycle is \(f_s/f\). Cycles per sample is \(f/f_s\). Normalised angular frequency is \(2\pi f/f_s\) radians per sample.
