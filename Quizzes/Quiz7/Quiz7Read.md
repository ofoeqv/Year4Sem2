## Question 1
A Fabry Perot laser has the following design features. Select all correct answers.

A. The refractive indices of the p and n layers are lower than the refractive index in the active region. **[correct]**
B. The surface from which light is emitted is polished. **[correct]**
C. The length of the laser is designed so that it is equal to Nlambda/3.
D. All Fabry Perot lasers produce a single mode emission.
E. The threshold current is reduced when a thick active region is used.

Feedback: The length condition should be Nlambda/2, not Nlambda/3. Fabry Perot lasers can produce multimode emission. A thin active region reduces the threshold current.

## Question 2
Which of the following statements are false for the vertical cavity surface emitting laser (VCSEL)?

A. Optical power produced by a single VCSEL is lower than the Fabry Perot laser.
B. Thick layer of active region is used without increasing the threshold current. **[correct false statement]**
C. Charge injection is more efficient than the Fabry Perot laser. **[correct false statement]**
D. The distributed Bragg reflector (DBR) reduces the emission spectral width.
E. At high injection current, lasing efficiency reduces.

Feedback: Optical power from a single VCSEL is normally lower than a Fabry Perot laser because of the small active volume. DBRs reduce emission spectral width because they reflect the chosen wavelength. At high injection current, lasing efficiency reduces due to increased heat.

## Question 3
Quantum dot laser offers the following advantages over the bulk laser. Select all correct answers.

A. Better heat dissipation.
B. Lower crystal defects.
C. Wider emission linewidth.
D. Narrower emission linewidth. **[correct]**
E. Lower threshold current. **[correct]**

Feedback: Better heat dissipation is not true because heat dissipation depends on the thermal conductivity of the whole laser. Quantum dots produce a very narrow emission spectrum. Growth of quantum dots is more challenging using lattice-mismatched materials, so it is not likely to produce lower crystal defects.

## Question 4
Recommend the combination of narrow and wide bandgap materials that you would use to create a double heterojunction laser with an emission wavelength of 1550 nm.

A. Ge and GaAs
B. In0.53Ga0.47As and GaAs
C. In0.1Ga0.90As and GaAs
D. In0.53Ga0.47As and InP **[correct]**
E. Ge and Si

Feedback: Only a direct bandgap semiconductor material with a sufficiently small bandgap and a lattice matched substrate should be selected.

## Question 5
Semiconductors such as InAs and InSb do not make efficient lasers at room temperature because:

A. They have direct bandgaps.
B. Their Auger recombination is dominant at room temperature. **[correct]**
C. They have high electron mobilities.
D. Their lattice constants are large.
E. They have small electron effective masses.

Feedback: Auger recombination is a non-radiative recombination process and is dominant in narrow bandgap semiconductors like InAs and InSb. This is the main reason they are not used as efficient lasers at room temperature.

## Question 6
The refractive indices of the p and n cladding layers of a Fabry Perot laser with a GaAs active layer can be changed by selecting all correct answers.

A. Reducing the width of the Fabry Perot laser.
B. Increasing the doping concentrations in the p and n layers. **[correct]**
C. Increasing the thickness of the p and n AlxGa1-xAs layers.
D. Using p and n AlxGa1-xAs layers with higher Al compositions. **[correct]**

Feedback: The refractive index can be modified by changing the bandgap or the doping concentration.
