## Question 1
In an n-type semiconductor, Nc is the effective density of states in the conduction band. Assuming Nc = 1.1 x 10^19 cm^-3 and an n-type dopant concentration of 24.9 x 10^16 cm^-3 is incorporated into this semiconductor, calculate the room temperature Fermi level.

Provide your answer in eV, relative to the bottom of the conduction band Ec. You may assume complete ionization of the n-type dopant. Round your answer to 3 decimal places.

Answer: -0.098 eV **[correct]**

Accepted range: -0.098 ± 10%

Feedback/formula:

EF = EC + kT[ln(n/Nc) + (1/8)(n/Nc)]

kT = 0.026 eV at room temperature T = 300 K. The negative answer shows EF is below EC.

## Question 2
In an n-type semiconductor the Fermi level can be approximated by:

EF = EC + kT[ln(n/NC) + (1/8)(n/NC)]

where EC is the conduction band minima, n is the electron concentration and NC is the effective density of states in the conduction band.

Assuming NC = 2.86 x 10^19 cm^-3 in Si, calculate the Fermi level at 300 K if an n-type dopant concentration of 10^15 cm^-3 is incorporated into Si. You may assume complete ionization of the n-type dopant.

A. 0.267 eV above the conduction band minima
B. 0.267 eV below the conduction band minima **[correct]**
C. 0.167 eV above the conduction band minima
D. 0.167 eV below the conduction band minima
E. 0.167 eV above the midgap energy level

Feedback: Since n is much smaller than Nc, EF is expected to be below EC.

## Question 3
Which of the following two statements are true?

A. The Fermi level corresponds to the probability of electron occupancy of 0.5 at a given temperature. **[correct]**
B. The Fermi level in an n-type semiconductor is dependent on the electron concentration. **[correct]**
C. The Fermi level defines the probability of generating an electron-hole pair at room temperature.
D. The Fermi level corresponds to the energy required to promote an electron from the valence to the conduction band.

Feedback: Fermi level does not define probability of generating an electron-hole pair. Fermi level does not correspond to the energy required to promote an electron from valence to conduction band. The minimum energy to achieve this is the bandgap energy.

## Question 4
Select two correct answers about the intrinsic carrier concentration in a semiconductor.

A. Increases exponentially with increasing temperature. **[correct]**
B. Decreases exponentially with increasing bandgap. **[correct]**
C. Increases exponentially with increasing bandgap.
D. Decreases exponentially with increasing temperature.

Feedback: The intrinsic carrier concentration is proportional to exp(-Eg/2kT).

## Question 5
Consider an energy level E, which is higher than the Fermi level EF. Which of the following is true? Select one answer.

A. The Fermi Dirac distribution F(E) increases with E.
B. The Fermi Dirac distribution F(E) increases with temperature. **[correct]**
C. The Fermi Dirac distribution F(E) is independent of carrier concentration.
D. The Fermi Dirac distribution F(E) decreases linearly with E.

Feedback: F(E) decreases exponentially with E. Since the Fermi energy level EF is dependent on carrier concentration, F(E) is also dependent on carrier concentration.

## Question 6
Which of the following dopant is suitable for Si? Select one correct answer.

A. Phosphorus (P) can be used as an n-type dopant. **[correct]**
B. Boron (B) can be used as an n-type dopant.
C. Carbon (C) can be used as a p-type dopant.
D. Oxygen (O) is commonly used as an n-type dopant.
E. Zinc (Zn) is commonly used as an n-type dopant.

Feedback: Normally group V elements such as As and P are used as n-type dopants while group III elements such as B and In can be used as p-type dopants in Si.

## Question 7
Select two suitable dopants for GaAs.

A. Si is commonly used as the n-type dopant. **[correct]**
B. Be is commonly used as the p-type dopant. **[correct]**
C. B is commonly used as the p-type dopant.
D. O can be used as a p-type dopant.
E. N can be an n-type dopant.

Feedback: B is from group III and N is from group V. Hence they cannot be used as dopants in GaAs. O is not a suitable dopant as it has a large ionization energy.

## Question 8
Which of the following two statements are true?

A. Increasing dopant concentration will reduce the conductivity of the semiconductor.
B. Increasing the dopant concentration will reduce the resistivity of the semiconductor. **[correct]**
C. Increasing the n-type dopants will increase the Fermi level in the n-type semiconductor. **[correct]**
D. Increasing the n-type dopant concentration will increase the electron mobility.

Feedback: Increasing the n-type dopants does not increase the electron mobility, because mu = q tau / m*. Increasing the n-type dopants will increase the conductivity and therefore reduce the resistivity of the n-type semiconductor.

## Question 9
For a moderately doped Si pn junction, the dominant recombination process is:

A. Shockley Read Hall (SRH) **[correct]**
B. Auger
C. Radiation
D. Impact ionisation

Feedback: Tunnelling is not a carrier recombination process. Impact ionisation is not a carrier recombination process. Radiative recombination is not dominant because Si is an indirect bandgap semiconductor.

## Question 10
Which of the following two statements are true?

A. The SRH is a dominant radiative recombination in GaAs.
B. The SRH is a dominant non-radiative recombination process in GaAs. **[correct]**
C. The Auger recombination is the main dominant process in widebandgap materials such as AlGaAs and GaP.
D. The Auger recombination is an important process in narrow bandgap semiconductors such as InSb and HgCdTe. **[correct]**

Question 10 correct answers: B and D.
